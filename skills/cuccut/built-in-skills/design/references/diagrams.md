# Diagrams: flowcharts, org charts, system maps, swimlanes, sequence diagrams, timelines

**Build diagrams from separate elements, never as one `<svg>`.** The editor selects and drags an `<svg>` as one block, so in a flowchart drawn as a single svg no one can move a single box or edit its text. Guidance you may have loaded that hand-draws diagrams as an inline `<svg>` (in a `<figure>`) is for ordinary pages, not designs. One `<svg>` is still right for parts no one will move or retype: icons, logos, illustrations, the plot area of a data chart (with labels and callouts people will edit layered on top as separate elements). Steps laid out in a row on a page and joined only by chevrons still use a flex row.

## Host container

Diagrams are the one exception to the flex or grid rule: arrow values come from the px positions of the cards they connect, and flex children have no such positions. Everything goes in **one** host container: a `<div>` with `position: relative` and a px `width` and `height`, in which every element is placed with inline `position: absolute; left; top` (px). When the diagram fills the whole artboard, the host container **is** the artboard's root and the title is just one more positioned element (so every card and arrow is selectable with one click); inset every element 64px from the root's edges, since padding does not move positioned elements.

Write every element as literal markup: cards generated by `<sc-for>`, or cards whose `left`/`top` are `{{hole}}`s, cannot be dragged individually (a click selects all the copies). File order is paint order and click order; when two boxes overlap, the later one receives the click: write background bands first, then all arrows, then the cards, then the labels.

## Elements

- **Card**: a `<div>` with inline `width`, `height` and `box-sizing: border-box` (the arrow formulas use the outer box size), with its label inside, using only text and inline tags (`<b>`, `<span>`, `<br>`), never child `<div>`s: that way one click selects the card and dragging moves card and label together. A card is a flex box, so a label containing tags goes inside **one** `<span>`, as in the example; left loose, the parts sit side by side and lose their spaces. An icon goes inside the card before that `<span>`, with `gap: 8px` on the card (so the text can be selected on its own; to drag the card, grab its icon or padding). Size each kind of card once, by its longest label after wrapping (about 9px per character at 16px, 11px for capitals, 24px per line, plus padding), rounded to an even number; if the diagram does not fit, enlarge the artboard, never shrink the text. A decision diamond is a card about twice as wide and tall as its label, with `clip-path: polygon(50% 0, 100% 50%, 50% 100%, 0 50%)` and a fill, no border (the clip cuts borders off); its four corners are the midpoints of the card's sides. Never use a rotated square.
- **Arrow**: one small `<svg>` per **straight** segment, copied from the example, every attribute included. It is exactly the svg the editor's Arrow tool draws: selectable and draggable on its own, with a handle at each end. The svg covers the whole arrow from tail to **tip**; the path stops 2 `stroke-width`s short of the tip, under the arrowhead. Color, width and dashes go in the svg's `style` (`stroke`, `stroke-width`, `stroke-dasharray: 6 6`); the head itself follows the arrow's color and width and is never dashed (Safari draws the head from its own path instead: write the arrow's color in its `fill` attribute and in the first `fill` in its `style`). The Properties panel may write the user's changes as `stroke` and `stroke-width` attributes on the arrow's `<path>`: those take precedence, so change styles there. After changing the width, move each end of the path that has a filled head so its tip stays put. Each arrow carries its own `<defs>`, whose marker `id` is unlike any other marker in the file (`dc-arrow-head-filled-1`, `-2`…, continuing from existing numbers) and is named by its `marker-end`: hiding or deleting one arrow never changes another's head. Users can set either end to none, open or filled: heads already in the file stay as they are. A plain line is the same svg without `<defs>` and `marker-end`.
- **Elbow arrow**: a chain of straight segments, each starting exactly where the previous one ends, with a head only on the last (a single path with several points, or a curve, is selected as one box and stretches when scaled).
- **One-to-many**: run a line from the parent to the middle of the gap, draw a line across there from the first child's center x to the last child's center x, then run arrows from that line into each child (in an org chart use plain lines ending **on** the edge). Many-to-one is its mirror.
- **Labels on arrows** ("yes", "retry"): a separate small `<div>`, `font-size: 13px; line-height: 16px`, 8px or more from every line and card: above a line at y, its `top` is y − 28, below it y + 12; beside a vertical line at x, its `left` is x + 12 and its `top` is the segment midpoint − 8. "yes" or "no" goes at the end of the segment nearest the decision it belongs to. Its box also receives clicks, so keep it no wider than the text: a `width` just wider than the text (about 8px per character), `text-align: center`, `left` = the midpoint of its gap minus half the width.
- **Swimlanes or other background bands**: an empty `<div>`, written first, with its name as a separate label in a corner, clear of every line and card. Lifelines and timeline axes are plain lines.

## Values

Before writing the file, fix every card's `left`, `top`, `width` and `height` on a grid: cards in a row share a center y, cards in a column share a center x, gaps of 48px or more (96 or more when a line runs along the gap; label width + 16 when a label sits in the gap), all in whole px. An arrow leaves from the midpoint of one card's edge and its tip stops 1px short of the midpoint of the other card's edge: the right edge's midpoint is (`left + width`, `top + height/2`), the bottom edge's midpoint is (`left + width/2`, `top + height`). Place cards so each arrow has an edge of its own; two arrows that must share an edge (there and back between adjacent cards) go at one third and two thirds of that edge, at the same y (or x) on both cards. A loop with no gap or empty cell to pass through goes around the outside: out of a free edge, along a clear band 48px outside the cards, into a free edge of the earlier card. Keep segments horizontal or vertical, running through gaps or empty cells, never across cards.

Each svg is exactly as long as its segment, tail to tip, 8px thick, with the line in the middle. Let (x, y) be the segment's **left** end (its **top** end when vertical), whichever way it points; n its length; h is 2 `stroke-width`s when the segment ends in a head (4 in the example), 0 without one:

- Horizontal: `left: x; top: y − 4`, `width="n" height="8"`, path `M 0 4 L n−h 4` pointing right, `M n 4 L h 4` pointing left
- Vertical: `left: x − 4; top: y`, `width="8" height="n"`, path `M 4 0 L 4 n−h` pointing down, `M 4 n L 4 h` pointing up

The 1px comes off the tip end, so for an arrow pointing left or up, its box starts 1px from the card it points at. The `width`/`height` attributes, the `viewBox` and the `width`/`height` in the style are always the same two numbers.

**Arrows do not follow cards.** When you move or resize a card, recompute every arrow connected to it; users have to do the same when editing by hand, so keep turns few.
